Drywall Repair Guide: Fixing Holes, Cracks, and Water Damage

4S
By 4 Seasons Right Services Inc
August 13, 2026
Bellevue, WA
8 Min Read

Quick Guide Overview

  • Root Causes: Northwest settling, seismic shifts, moisture leaks, and everyday impacts drive 90% of drywall damage.
  • Water Damage Rule: Never just paint over a stain — identify leaks, excise soaked drywall, and dry backing studs to stop mold.
  • Texture Matching: Orange peel, knockdown, and smooth finishes require feathered joint compound coats for seamless blends.

Drywall damage rarely shows up as one big dramatic event. It's a doorknob dent that's been there for two years. A hairline crack over a doorway that slowly gets longer. A brownish stain on the ceiling that you keep meaning to look at. None of it feels urgent — until you're ready to sell the house, host a get-together, or repaint, and suddenly every imperfection is impossible to unsee.

At 4 Seasons Right Services Inc., based in Bellevue, WA, drywall repair is one of the most common calls we get, and for good reason: it's one of the most common types of home damage there is. This guide breaks down the three big categories — holes, cracks, and water damage — what causes each one, how they're actually fixed, and when it's worth calling in a professional instead of grabbing a tub of spackle.

Why Drywall Gets Damaged in the First Place

Drywall is durable for everyday use, but it's not indestructible. The most common causes we see in Bellevue homes include:

Everyday impacts

Doorknobs, furniture moves, kids, pets, and moving boxes.

Settling and seismic activity

The Pacific Northwest sees regular minor seismic activity, and homes naturally settle over time, both of which stress drywall seams and corners.

Humidity and moisture

Our wet climate means roof leaks, plumbing leaks, and condensation issues show up on drywall faster than in drier regions.

Poor original installation

Improperly taped seams or under-fastened panels crack and separate well before they should.

Age and structural shifting

Older homes, especially those with original plaster-and-lath or early drywall, are more prone to cracking as materials shift over decades.

Understanding the cause matters because it determines whether a repair is truly cosmetic or whether it's a symptom of something bigger — a slow leak, a structural shift, or a ventilation problem.

Part 1: Fixing Holes in Drywall

Drywall holes range from tiny picture hanging pinholes to large gouges through studs. Here is the breakdown by size:

Small Holes Nail Holes, Screw Holes, Small Dings

These are the easiest fix and often genuinely DIY-friendly:

  1. Clean the area of loose debris and loose paper backing
  2. Apply lightweight spackle or joint compound with a flexible putty knife
  3. Let it dry fully, then sand smooth with fine-grit sandpaper
  4. Prime with drywall primer and paint to match sheen and color
Medium Holes Doorknob Dents, Punch-Through Holes (1–4 inches)

Once a hole is bigger than about 1–2 inches, spackle alone won't hold. These typically need:

  1. A self-adhesive mesh patch or a "California patch" (a scrap of drywall cut slightly larger than the hole, backed with a strip of wood or drywall for support)
  2. Joint compound applied in thin coats over the patch
  3. Multiple coats, each sanded, to feather the edges into the surrounding wall
  4. Primer and paint matched to the surrounding finish and sheen
Large Holes Multiple Inches, Structural Damage, Multiple Studs Involved

Large holes — from anything like a doorknob punching all the way through, water damage removal, or accidental impact — usually require:

  1. Cutting a clean rectangular section back to the nearest wall studs
  2. Installing wood backing backers and a new drywall patch fastened directly to framing
  3. Taping all seams with paper or fiberglass mesh tape
  4. Multiple coats of joint compound, feathered wide to blend into the existing wall
  5. Sanding, priming, and matching texture (orange peel / knockdown) and paint sheen
This is where DIY results often fall short — not because the hole doesn't get covered, but because uneven texture and visible seams show up clearly once paint goes on.

Part 2: Fixing Drywall Cracks

Not all cracks are equal, and reading them correctly matters.

Hairline Cracks

Thin, cosmetic cracks — often near corners, doorways, or where two pieces of drywall meet — are usually caused by normal home settling or minor seasonal movement. These are typically repaired by:

  • Widening the crack slightly with a utility knife (this "V-groove" helps compound bond better)
  • Applying paper or fiberglass mesh tape over the crack
  • Covering with joint compound in thin, feathered coats
  • Sanding and priming before repainting

Corner Cracks

Cracks at inside or outside corners often mean the corner bead or tape has separated. These need the corner material reset or replaced, not just filled, or the crack will reopen within months.

Recurring or Widening Cracks

If a crack keeps coming back after repair, or if it's growing wider over time, that's a signal worth paying attention to. It can point to:

  • Foundation settling
  • Structural movement
  • Undersized or improperly installed framing
  • Nearby seismic stress

In these cases, patching the crack without addressing the underlying movement is a short-term fix. A contractor can help determine whether it's routine settling (very common and usually not serious) or something that warrants a closer structural look.

Part 3: Fixing Water-Damaged Drywall

Water damage is the category where "just patch it" advice can actually cause problems down the road, because what you can see is rarely the full extent of the damage.

Identifying Water-Damaged Drywall

Yellow, brown, or copper-colored stains
Sagging or bulging in ceiling or wall
A soft, spongy feel when pressed
Peeling paint or bubbling texture
Musty, damp odor in the room
Visible dark mold spots

Why Water-Damaged Drywall Needs Special Handling

Unlike a hole or a crack, water damage often means the drywall's structural integrity is compromised — even if the surface still looks mostly intact. Wet drywall loses strength, can harbor mold within 24–48 hours in the right conditions, and often signals a leak that's still active somewhere behind the wall.

The Right Repair Process for Water Damage

1
Find and stop the source: Repairing drywall before fixing the leak (roof, plumbing, window flashing, or a failed gutter) just means paying for the repair twice.
2
Remove all compromised material: Cut out beyond the visibly damaged area, since moisture spreads further than surface staining suggests.
3
Check for mold and address it properly: Clean and sanitize the framing or conduct involved remediation depending on moisture extent.
4
Allow the area to fully dry: Air out framing behind the drywall thoroughly with industrial fans or dehumidifiers.
5
Replace insulation if saturated: Wet fiberglass loses its R-value and traps mold against newly installed drywall.
6
Install new drywall, tape, & mud: Hang fresh sheetrock, tape seams, apply feathered mud coats, and match wall or ceiling textures.
7
Prime with stain-blocking primer: Essential to prevent residual water stains from bleeding through new paint.

DIY vs. Professional Drywall Repair: How to Decide

The honest reality is that most small, isolated holes and hairline cracks are within reach for a confident DIYer. Where professional repair earns its cost is texture matching, sheen matching, water damage (where hidden extent and mold risk matter), and any repair visible in strong light.

Situation DIY Often Works Call a Professional
Small nail holes, dings Yes —
Doorknob-sized hole Sometimes If texture/sheen matching matters
Hairline settling crack Sometimes If it recurs or widens
Multiple large holes or cracks — Yes
Any water-damaged drywall — Yes, especially with mold risk
Ceiling drywall repairs — Usually yes (overhead blending is tough)
Textured walls (orange peel, knockdown) — Yes, texture matching is hard by hand

How Much Does Drywall Repair Cost?

Costs vary based on damage size, number of repairs, texture complexity, and whether painting is included, but general ranges homeowners in the Bellevue area can expect:

  • Small holes & hairline cracks: Typically the most affordable, often bundled if multiple small repairs are completed in one service visit.
  • Medium repairs (doorknob holes, several feet of cracking): Moderate cost, largely driven by texture matching and drying time between coats.
  • Large holes or water damage repairs: Highest cost, since they involve material removal, possible mold considerations, and full texture/paint matching.

The most reliable way to know your actual cost is a free in-person estimate, since drywall pricing depends heavily on texture type, ceiling height, and accessibility.

Preventing Future Drywall Damage

A few habits go a long way:

Install baseboard door stops to prevent dents
Address roof and plumbing leaks immediately
Run bathroom exhaust fans during/after showers
Check under sinks & water heaters for slow leaks
Keep gutters clean to prevent roofline overflow
Monitor recurring cracks over seasonal swings
